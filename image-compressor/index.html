How image compression works

Digital photos straight from a phone camera are usually far bigger than they need to be for the web. A single shot can weigh 4–8 MB, which makes web pages slow, eats email attachment limits and fills up storage. Compression re-encodes the image at a quality level you choose: the file gets smaller, and at sensible settings (75–85% quality) the difference is invisible to the eye.

This tool draws your image onto a browser canvas and re-saves it at your chosen quality and format. JPG is usually the best choice for photographs, WebP often goes smaller still, and PNG stays lossless — best for graphics and transparency, but it will not shrink a photo much. Move the slider, compress, and compare the before and after sizes and previews side by side; if the result looks soft, raise the quality and run it again. It costs nothing to experiment.

When should you compress an image?

  • Websites and blogs: lighter images load faster, which visitors and search engines both prefer.
  • Email and forms: many inboxes and upload forms cap attachments at a few megabytes.
  • Marketplaces and portals: listing sites often reject oversized photos — compressing beats being refused.
  • Storage: a compressed copy keeps the memories without the megabytes. Keep your original somewhere safe if it matters.

Need a different size as well as a smaller file? The image resizer changes the pixel dimensions, and the image converter switches formats — all in the same private, in-browser way. Our guide on how to compress an image without losing quality explains the settings in more depth.

Image compressor — frequently asked questions

Will compressing ruin my photo?

At 75–85% quality, most people cannot see any difference in a photograph. Lower settings save more space but can introduce softness or blocky patches, which is why the tool shows you a preview and the exact saving before you download.

Why did my PNG not get much smaller?

PNG compression is lossless, so savings are modest for photographs. For a big reduction on a photo, choose JPG or WebP as the output format. Keep PNG for graphics, screenshots and images that need a transparent background.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The compression runs in your browser on your device. Nothing is sent to a server — see our Privacy Policy.

Can I compress more than one image?

This tool handles one image at a time so you can judge the quality of each result. It is quick to repeat: download, then choose the next image.
