How to remove a background in Photoshop vs free online

Published 11 November 2026 · SnapFixy team · 4 minute read

Removing a background used to mean one thing: Photoshop. Today, a free online tool can do the same job in seconds. So is Photoshop still worth it, or has the free option caught up? The honest answer depends on the image — and on how often you do this.

The Photoshop way

Photoshop offers several ways to cut out a subject. The quickest is Select Subject: open your image, click the button, and Photoshop's AI draws a selection around the main subject. For a clean portrait or product shot, that is often enough. For anything trickier — wispy hair, a glass bottle, a subject against a busy background — you move into Select and Mask, painting over the edges with the Refine Edge brush until the selection looks right.

The catch is everything around the tool. Photoshop is a subscription, currently around £22 a month for the Photography plan in the UK, and it needs a computer powerful enough to run it. It also has a learning curve: Select and Mask is genuinely good, but only once you know which sliders to touch. For a professional retoucher doing this all day, that control is the whole point. For someone who needs one clean cut-out for a marketplace listing, it is a lot of machinery for a small job.

The free online way

Free online background removers work in two flavours. The common one uploads your photo to a server, runs an AI model there, and sends back a transparent PNG. It is fast and works on any device, but a copy of your photo leaves your hands — worth thinking about for a headshot, a child's photo, or an unreleased product.

The second flavour runs the AI in your browser instead. The model downloads once, the cut-out happens on your own device, and the photo is never uploaded anywhere. That is how SnapFixy's background remover works: the first use takes a little longer while the model downloads (roughly 40 MB), and after that each image takes a few seconds — with no account, no watermark and no subscription.

Photoshop vs free online, side by side

  • Cost: Photoshop is a monthly subscription; a free online tool costs nothing. A few cut-outs a month saves you over £250 a year.
  • Speed: A free tool wins for a single image — open the page, drop the photo in, download the result. Photoshop wins when batch-processing dozens of images with recorded actions.
  • Quality: For everyday images — portraits, products, pets, logos — the results are usually indistinguishable. Photoshop pulls ahead on the hardest images: fine hair against clutter, glass and transparent objects, or subjects that blend into the background.
  • Privacy: A browser-based free tool keeps your photo on your device. Upload-based tools may keep copies on their servers, so check the policy first.
  • Skill needed: A free tool needs none; Photoshop rewards practice. If you just want the cut-out, that control is friction.

When Photoshop is still the right call

Choose Photoshop when the image is genuinely difficult and the result has to be perfect — a hero image for a paying client, a print catalogue, or a composite where several cut-outs must blend seamlessly. It is also the right call if you already pay for it and edit photos regularly.

When the free option wins

Choose a free online tool when the job is routine: a product photo for eBay or Etsy, a profile picture, a slide deck, a social post, or a school project. The whole job, from opening the page to downloading the PNG, is often under a minute — and with nothing to install, it works just as well on a borrowed laptop or a phone.
