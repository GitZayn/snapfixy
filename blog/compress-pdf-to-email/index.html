How to compress a PDF so you can email it

Published 21 November 2026 · SnapFixy team · 5 minute read

You have finished the document, attached it to an email, and the mail app refuses it: file too large. Most inboxes cap attachments at around 10–25 MB, and a PDF full of scans or photographs sails past that without trying. The fix is to make the PDF smaller — but what actually makes a PDF smaller depends on why it is big in the first place, and the honest tools will tell you that.

This guide walks through it plainly: what shrinks a PDF, what does not, and the steps that get a file under the limit without uploading a sensitive document to a stranger's server.

Why PDFs get too big for email

A PDF is a container, and its size comes from what is inside it. Two things dominate: the images — photographs and scanned pages are the heavyweights — and the file's internal structure, which can carry duplicated objects and leftover data from the software that wrote it. A ten-page report of plain text is tiny. The same ten pages as colour scans can be many times larger. Knowing which one you are dealing with decides what will actually help.

What actually makes a PDF smaller

There are two honest levers. The first is tidier structure: re-saving the document with compressed object streams packs its internal packaging more efficiently, which helps files written by older scanners and office software. The second is less content: fewer pages, or smaller images. What does not work is wishing — no tool can make the same pages with the same images dramatically smaller without changing how they look, and any service that promises exactly that is quietly downscaling your images on its servers, which also means you uploaded the document.

The quick way: compress it in your browser

Your files are never uploaded. The optimisation runs entirely in your browser, so even a sensitive document stays on your device.

  1. Open the PDF compressor. It works in any modern browser, on a computer or a phone.
  2. Drop your PDF in or click to browse. Scanned documents and files from older office software benefit most.
  3. Let the tool re-save it. Your browser rebuilds the document with compressed object streams.
  4. Check the before and after sizes before you download — and keep your original if the saving is not worth it.

When compression alone is not enough

If your PDF is large because it is full of high-resolution scans or photographs, re-saving its structure will only help a little — the images are the weight, and this tool does not downscale them. The practical fixes are to send less or rebuild smaller. Use the PDF split tool to extract only the pages the recipient needs: a ten-page extract of a sixty-page report is far smaller to send, and it avoids sharing pages that were never meant to leave your desk. Or, if you still have the source material, run the photos through the image compressor first and build the document again from the smaller images.

Before you hit send

  • Check the size against your provider's limit — most inboxes cap attachments at around 10–25 MB.
  • Open the compressed file and check every page looks right, especially pages with signatures or form fields.
  • If the file is still over the limit, split out the pages you need rather than sending the whole document.
  • Keep the original somewhere safe — compressed copies are for sending, not for archiving.

And if the file simply will not fit under the limit, remember that email is not the only way to send it. Uploading the PDF to a cloud drive and sharing a link gets the same document to the same person without any size limit — and for a file that must arrive unchanged, a link beats a squeezed attachment every time. Compression is the right answer when the file is just over the limit; when it is far over, sending less or sharing a link is the honest fix.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting a service that promises a huge reduction. It is almost certainly downscaling your images on its servers — and you had to upload the document.
  • Compressing the only copy. Keep the original; the compressed version is for sending.
  • Sending the whole sixty-page document when one page was asked for. Split it first.
  • Assuming a PDF is small because it has few pages. One scanned page can outweigh fifty pages of text.

Quick questions

How much smaller will my PDF get? It depends entirely on how the file was made. Structurally bloated PDFs often shrink by a useful amount. PDFs dominated by large images may barely change, and the tool will show you the true figures before you download anything.

Will the quality of my document change? No. The pages, text and images are preserved exactly; only the internal packaging of the file is made more efficient. Nothing is downscaled or re-encoded.

Is it safe to compress a PDF with signatures? The pages are preserved exactly, but as with any tool that re-saves a PDF, interactive form fields and digital signatures can behave differently afterwards. Check important documents before you send them on.

Is my PDF uploaded? Never. The optimisation runs in your browser using a PDF library loaded into the page.
