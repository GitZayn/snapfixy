How to split a PDF into separate pages

Published 21 November 2026 · SnapFixy team · 5 minute read

A PDF is often bigger than the job needs. A sixty-page report arrives by email, and the form you are filling in wants one page of it. A bank statement covers a year, and the letting agent wants a single month. A scanner has saved an entire folder of documents as one file, and you need them as separate ones. Splitting a PDF — pulling out the pages you actually need and saving them as a new, smaller file — is the answer, and it takes about a minute.

You do not need to install anything, and you do not need to upload the document to a stranger's server. SnapFixy's PDF split tool runs in your browser: your file stays on your device, the pages you choose are copied into a brand-new PDF, and your original is never changed.

What splitting a PDF actually does

Splitting is not editing in the usual sense. Nothing is retyped, reformatted or re-scanned: the tool reads your PDF, copies the pages you asked for — in the order you listed them — and saves them as a new document. The text, images and layout of those pages are preserved exactly. What changes is only which pages are in the file. The original stays exactly as it was, which means there is no risk in trying again if the first extract is not quite right.

The quick way: split it in your browser

Your files are never uploaded. The whole job runs in your browser, and your original PDF is never changed.

  1. Open the PDF split tool. It works in any modern browser, on a computer or a phone.
  2. Drop your PDF in or click to browse. The tool counts its pages and tells you how many there are.
  3. Type the pages you want. A single number (2), a range (1-4), or a mixture (1-3, 7, 10-12) — separated by commas.
  4. Extract. Your browser copies exactly those pages into a brand-new PDF.
  5. Download the extract and check it before you send it on.

Page ranges, explained

The page box accepts a small language of its own, and it is worth learning because it covers every splitting job there is:

  • One page: type the number — 5 gives you page five only.
  • A range: 1-4 gives you the first four pages.
  • A mixture: 1-3, 7, 10-12 gives you pages 1, 2, 3, 7, 10, 11 and 12.
  • Out of order: 5, 1-2 gives you page 5 first, then pages 1 and 2 — the extract follows the order you list, not the original order.
  • One page at a time: to split a document into individual pages, extract one page per run.

Jobs this is made for

  • One page for a form: a signed page, a certificate, or a single invoice from a long statement.
  • Separating scans: a scanner that saved everything as one PDF can be separated into individual documents.
  • Trimming before you merge: pull the relevant pages from several documents, then combine them with PDF merge.
  • Sharing safely: send one page instead of the whole document, so pages that were never meant for the recipient never leave your desk.

What to check after splitting

Open the new file and check the pages are the ones you wanted, in the order you wanted. Interactive extras such as bookmarks and clickable links are not guaranteed to carry over, so if the document relies on them, check before you send it on. And remember the original is untouched — if the extract is wrong, just run it again.

It also pays to name the new file clearly before you send it. A file called "contract-pages-3-5.pdf" tells the recipient exactly what it is, while "document.pdf" does not — and when a form asks for one page, a clear filename is the difference between your file being opened first and being asked for again. Keep the original's name close, so you can always tell which file the extract came from.

Common mistakes

  • Asking for a page that does not exist. Check the page count first — it is easy to ask for page 12 of a ten-page document.
  • Forgetting the extract follows your list order, not the original order.
  • Sending the whole document anyway. The point of splitting is to send less.
  • Splitting a password-protected PDF. Encrypted files cannot be opened in the browser; remove the protection in the application that created the file first, if you are entitled to do so.

Quick questions

Can I split a PDF into individual pages? Yes. Extract one page at a time — for example, pages 1, 2 and 3 as three separate runs — and each becomes its own PDF. For a long document, work through the pages in batches.

Will splitting change my original PDF? No. The tool reads your original and builds a new file from the pages you chose. Your original stays exactly as it was.

What if a PDF is password-protected? Encrypted PDFs cannot be opened by the browser library, so they cannot be split here. Remove the password with the software that created the file first, if you are entitled to do so.

Can I extract pages out of order? Yes — the pages appear in the new PDF in the order you list them, so 5, 1-2 gives you page 5 first, then pages 1 and 2.
