Best image sizes for social media in 2026

Published 25 November 2026 · SnapFixy team · 4 minute read

Post a photo at the wrong size and the platform will fix it for you — by cropping off the edges, stretching it, or squeezing it down until the text is unreadable. Every network has its own preferred dimensions, and they change more often than you would expect: Instagram's grid went tall in 2025, YouTube now recommends larger thumbnails than the old 1280 × 720 standard, and LinkedIn has changed its Page cover size. This guide lists the sizes that work in 2026, platform by platform — and the three sizes that cover almost everything.

The three sizes that cover almost everything

Export just three versions of an image and you can post it almost anywhere without cropping or blur:

  • 1080 × 1080 (1:1, square) — the safe default for Facebook, Instagram and X feeds.
  • 1080 × 1350 (4:5, portrait) — the tallest feed image most platforms accept, so it takes up the most screen space.
  • 1080 × 1920 (9:16, vertical) — full-screen size for Stories, Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

Always start from the largest original you have and shrink down, never the other way round — and keep text and faces in the middle of the frame, because the edges are the first thing a platform crops.

Instagram

  • Profile photo: 320 × 320. Displays at about 110 × 110, cropped to a circle — keep the face or logo centred.
  • Feed, square: 1080 × 1080.
  • Feed, portrait: 1080 × 1350 (4:5) — the best choice for most posts.
  • Feed, tall: 1080 × 1440 (3:4), for posts from the Instagram app, matching the taller grid.
  • Feed, landscape: 1080 × 566.
  • Stories and Reels: 1080 × 1920 (9:16). Keep text away from the top and bottom edges, where the interface sits.

Facebook

  • Profile photo: 320 × 320.
  • Cover photo: 851 × 315 — it crops on mobile, so keep text centred.
  • Feed photo: 1080 × 1080 (square) or 1080 × 1350 (portrait).
  • Link preview: 1200 × 630 (1.91:1).
  • Stories and Reels: 1080 × 1920 (9:16).

X (formerly Twitter)

  • Profile photo: 400 × 400.
  • Header: 1500 × 500.
  • In-feed image: 1600 × 900 (16:9), 1080 × 1080 square, or 1080 × 1350 portrait.
  • Link card: 1200 × 630.

LinkedIn

  • Profile photo: 400 × 400.
  • Personal background: 1584 × 396.
  • Company Page cover: 1512 × 256.
  • Page logo: 400 × 400.
  • Feed image: 1080 × 1350 (4:5) or 1080 × 1080 (1:1).
  • Link preview: 1200 × 627.

YouTube

  • Channel icon: 800 × 800.
  • Channel banner: 2560 × 1440. Keep text inside the central 1546 × 423 safe area.
  • Video thumbnail: 1280 × 720 minimum; newer guidance recommends 3840 × 2160 for the sharpest result.
  • Shorts thumbnail: 1080 × 1920 (9:16).

TikTok

  • Profile photo: 200 × 200.
  • Photo posts and video: 1080 × 1920 (9:16) — anything else leaves black bars.

Pinterest

  • Standard Pin: 1000 × 1500 (2:3) — Pinterest's recommended ratio.
  • Square Pin: 1000 × 1000.

Pins taller than 2:3 get cut off in the feed — 1000 × 1500 is the sweet spot.

Common mistakes

  • One square image everywhere. It wastes screen space on vertical feeds and gets cropped on Stories.
  • Uploading a tiny image and letting the platform enlarge it. Upscaling always blurs — start from a large original.
  • Text near the edges. Circle crops, phone notches and interface buttons eat the edges first.
  • Forgetting the safe area — check a banner or profile photo on a phone, not just a desktop.
  • Saving everything as PNG. A photo saved as PNG can be ten times larger than the same photo as a JPG, for no visible benefit.

Quick questions

What is the best all-round size for social media? 1080 × 1350 (4:5). It works as a feed post on Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X and Threads.

What size should an Instagram post be in 2026? 1080 × 1350 (4:5) for a standard portrait post, or 1080 × 1440 (3:4) from the app. Stories and Reels are 1080 × 1920.

Do I need a different image for every platform? No. The three sizes at the top of this guide cover nearly every placement. Only profile photos, covers and banners need their own sizes.

Will resizing make my image blurry? Shrinking keeps an image sharp; enlarging does not. Always resize down from a larger original.
